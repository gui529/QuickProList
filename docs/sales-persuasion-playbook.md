## Canon — best-rated books (what to steal from each)

Ranked for **B2B / subscription / local SMB** outreach. Prefer **field-study** books for technique; **psychology** books for mechanism.

| Priority | Book | Author | Evidence | Use when |
|----------|------|--------|----------|----------|
| 1 | **Influence** (2021 expanded) | Robert Cialdini | Decades of experiments; 7 principles incl. **Unity** | Every message: which principles are active, ethically |
| 2 | **Pre-Suasion** | Robert Cialdini | Field + lab | *Before* the pitch: what you make salient (local, trade, scarcity) |
| 3 | **SPIN Selling** | Neil Rackham | 35k sales calls | Discovery calls, longer conversations, qualifying fit |
| 4 | **The Challenger Sale** | Dixon & Adamson | 6k+ rep study | Reframe: teach something new about their market, then tailor |

| 6 | **Never Split the Difference** | Chris Voss | FBI negotiation | Replies, objections, tone: labels, mirrors, calibrated questions |
| 7 | **Gap Selling** | Keenan | Practitioner | Current state → future state → gap; no feature dumps |
| 8 | **The Mom Test** | Rob Fitzpatrick | Practitioner | Avoid fake yes; talk about *their* life, not your idea |
| 9 | **Fanatical Prospecting** | Jeb Blount | Practitioner | Discipline: one city/trade, daily volume, no spray-and-pray blasts |
| 10 | **To Sell Is Human** | Daniel Pink | Field study | Attunement, buoyancy, clarity — service framing |
| 11 | **How to Win Friends and Influence People** | Dale Carnegie | Classic | Liking: names, genuine interest, no argument-winning |

## Frameworks — operational cheat sheet

### Cialdini’s 7 (ethical application)

| Principle | QuickProList lever | Avoid |
|-----------|-------------------|--------|
| **Reciprocity** | Free preview / enroll link before payment; useful local insight in email | Fake gifts, strings attached |
| **Commitment** | Micro-yes: “2-minute look” before “subscribe” | Big ask on first touch |
| **Social proof** | “Short list per category in {city}” if true; neighbor trades only with permission | Invented stats or fake logos |
| **Liking** | Human name, local specificity, respect their time | Flattery, fake familiarity |
| **Authority** | Domain, clear product; expertise = *local discovery*, not fake credentials | “As seen on…” without proof |
| **Scarcity** | Curated slots per city/category (only if enforced in product) | Fake countdown timers |
| **Unity** | “Fellow {city} businesses”, “home-service pros” shared identity | Us-vs-them or manipulative tribe bait |

**Pre-Suasion:** Open with what you want them to think about (local homeowners searching, curated list) *before* mentioning QuickProList or price.

### SPIN (Rackham) — discovery & longer threads

- **S**ituation — how they get jobs today (referrals, Google, Angi?)  
- **P**roblem — gaps (inconsistent leads, wrong leads, no time for marketing)  
- **I**mplication — cost of status quo (slow months, competitor visibility)  
- **N**eed-payoff — them describing value of being findable locally  

**Rule:** In **small** asks (cold email), use *implied* P/I in one line; save full SPIN for phone/DM.

### Challenger — Teach, Tailor, Take control

1. **Teach** — one insight they didn’t have (e.g. homeowners search by city+category; lead auctions vs curated list).  
2. **Tailor** — tie to *their* business name, city, trade.  
3. **Take control** — recommend a next step (“see your preview” not “let me know if interested”).

### JOLT — when they stall (not “no,” but “not now”)

- **Judge** indecision vs objection  
- **Offer** a recommendation (“I’d start with the preview link”)  
- **Limit** scope (one city, one category)  
- **Take risk off** — trial, cancel anytime, unsubscribe  

### Voss — tactical empathy (replies & calls)

- **Label** emotions: “Sounds like you’re slammed with field work.”  
- **Mirror** last 1–3 words.  
- **Calibrated questions:** “How am I supposed to help you decide?” / “What would need to be true for this to be worth two minutes?”  
- **No** — get them to say no safely; “Is it a ridiculous idea to see the preview?”  
- **Accusation audit** upfront: “You probably get a lot of marketing email…”

### Mom Test

- Bad: “Would you pay $29.99 for a listing?”  
- Good: “How do homeowners usually find you last month?” / “What happened the last time you tried Angi?”  
- Compliments are lies; commitments (click, call, trial) are signal.

### Gap Selling (Keenan)

`Current state → Future state → Gap → Our fit in the gap`  
Never lead with features; lead with **gap** (visibility when people search `{category} in {city}`).

### Carnegie (short)

- Use their **name** (business + owner if known).  
- Talk in terms of **their** interests.  
- Let them save face; never “you’re wrong about marketing.”

### Kahneman (copy)

- **System 1:** short sentences, one CTA, concrete city/trade.  
- **System 2:** price and terms *after* interest or on enroll page.  
- **Loss aversion:** what they miss (searches in their town), not only what they gain.

## QuickProList ICP (local pros)

**Buyer:** Owner-operator or office manager, plumber/HVAC/electrician/etc., time-poor, skeptical of lead-gen scams.  
**Job to be done:** Be found by **local** homeowners without bidding wars or junk leads.  
**Alternatives:** Google Business Profile, Angi/Thumbtack, word of mouth, yard signs.  
**Our wedge:** Curated **pinned** listing on a simple local directory — not a lead auction. **$29.99/mo**, trial path when product supports it.

**Positioning line (adapt):** “A short, curated list of {category} pros homeowners see when they search in {City} — not a pay-per-lead marketplace.”

## Message architecture (cold email)

1. **Subject:** Plain, specific, curiosity or local relevance — no emoji, no fake `Re:`.  
2. **Line 1:** Them (city, trade, or situation) — not “We are QuickProList.”  
3. **Line 2–3:** Insight (Challenger teach) + low-pressure offer (reciprocity).  
4. **CTA:** One action — “See your listing preview” (enroll URL when city+category set).  
5. **Sign:** Real first name + role.  
6. **Footer:** Reason + unsubscribe (compliance); **defer price** to enroll/checkout when possible.

**Anti-patterns:** Feature lists, multiple CTAs, $29.99 in first screen for cold mail, “Act now”, ALL CAPS, competitor bashing without facts.

## Objections (starter responses — customize, stay honest)

| Objection | Angle |
|-----------|--------|
| “Too expensive” | Cost vs one job; compare to lead-gen spend; trial/cancel |
| “I get enough from referrals” | Referrals + capture people who don’t know anyone |
| “Is this a scam?” | Real domain, unsubscribe, no bank details in email, link to site |
| “I already have Google” | Complement; curated list for category search on QuickProList |
| “Not interested” | Graceful exit; suppress; no guilt trips |

Use Voss **labels** in replies; never argue.
